PAPER SUITCASES — WARA BLUE

(Verse 1)
Paper suitcases stitched with dust and prayer
We left the only land we knew, but the ghosts were waiting there
From Addis to Accra, from Lagos to Asmara's light
We carried histories in our palms into the freezing night
Lines drawn in rooms we never entered, borders in the sand
They carved up where our mothers wept and stole the fertile land
"Shh, my child, don't speak too loud, just keep your head down low,
We cross tonight, don't ask them why, just pack your bags and go."

(Chorus)
And they ask us why we're here, why we walk these cold concrete blocks
Why we trade the golden sun for these unyielding locks
We ran from wars we didn't start, from debts we didn't sign
But you built your empires on our backs, and drew the boundary line
Now we're carrying fractured hearts in a paper suitcase stream
Trying to build a sanctuary out of someone else's dream.

(Verse 2)
They gave us papers, gave us rules, but never gave us peace
Just a quiet kind of exile where the judgments never cease
Cold stares upon the morning train, the whispers in the hall
The polite words wrapped in policy that build a higher wall
We cleaned the floors, we laid the brick, we kept the engines turned
But they watch us like we're thieves inside the cities that we earned
And the homeland feels so distant now, a fading, watercolor shore
While we stand here in the shadows, eternal strangers at the door.

(Bridge)
Now my own child looks into my eyes and asks the very same
"Papa, why do they look at us like we don't belong to our name?"
I tell her stories of the riverbed, of trees that touch the sky
Of a place where our vocabulary isn't framed inside a 'why'
We are the seeds blown by the storm, but the roots are deep and strong
No matter what the papers say, this earth is where we belong.

(Outro)
Stitched with dust and prayer...
Paper suitcases...
We are still here.
We are still here.
